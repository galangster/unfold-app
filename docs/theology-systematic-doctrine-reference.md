## 1. The Eight Major Loci of Systematic Theology

### 1.1 Prolegomena (First Things / Theological Method)

**Key Questions:**
- How do we know God? What are the sources and methods of theology?
- What is revelation? How does general revelation differ from special revelation?
- What is the nature, authority, and sufficiency of Scripture?

**Main Positions:**
- **General Revelation:** God discloses His existence and attributes through creation (Rom 1:19-20), conscience (Rom 2:14-15), and providence. Sufficient to leave humanity "without excuse" but insufficient for salvation.
- **Special Revelation:** God's redemptive communication through Scripture and the person of Jesus Christ. The Bible is "breathed out by God" (2 Tim 3:16), inspired, authoritative, and sufficient.
- **Sola Scriptura:** Scripture alone is the final authority in matters of faith and practice.

**Restoration Movement Position:**
- **Bible-only authority.** Churches of Christ reject all formalized creeds, confessions, and catechisms on the grounds that they add to or detract from Scripture. The motto: "Where the Bible speaks, we speak; where the Bible is silent, we are silent."
- **No creed but Christ, no book but the Bible.** This is foundational to the movement. Theology is what humans say about the Bible; doctrine is simply what the Bible says.
- **Hermeneutical method:** Historically, Command, Example, and Necessary Inference (CENI) — if a teaching is found as an explicit command, an approved apostolic example, or a necessary inference from Scripture, it is binding. Some within the movement critique this as overly rigid.
- **Scripture is plain and sufficient.** The Bible provides an understandable "blueprint" for the church and for Christian living.

**How This Shows Up in Devotional Writing:**
- Always ground devotional content in specific Scripture passages with proper context.
- Never elevate tradition, creeds, or human opinions to the level of Scripture.
- When quoting or referencing extrabiblical sources (theologians, authors), frame them as commentary, not authority.
- Encourage the reader to "search the Scriptures" themselves (Acts 17:11).
- Use language like "The Bible teaches..." or "Scripture says..." rather than "The church teaches..." or "Theologians believe..."

### 1.2 Theology Proper (The Doctrine of God)

**Key Questions:**
- Who is God? What are His attributes (communicable and incommunicable)?
- What is the Trinity — one God in three persons?
- What is God's relationship to creation (creation ex nihilo, providence, sovereignty)?

**Main Positions on the Trinity:**
- **Orthodox Trinitarianism:** One God (one essence/substance), three distinct persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) — coeternal, coequal, consubstantial. Affirmed at Nicaea (325 AD), Constantinople (381 AD).
- **Modalism/Sabellianism (HERESY):** God is one person who appears in three successive modes (Father in OT, Son in incarnation, Spirit after Pentecost). Denies distinct persons.
- **Arianism (HERESY):** The Son is a created being, not fully divine. "There was a time when the Son was not."
- **Tritheism (HERESY):** Three separate Gods rather than one God in three persons.

**Restoration Movement Position:**
- **Trinitarian.** One God, three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. However, the movement has historically been cautious about the term "Trinity" itself — Alexander Campbell "rejected the term 'Trinity' but did not reject the theological idea of the tri-unity of the Christian God." The concept is affirmed; the extrabiblical terminology is held loosely.
- **Attributes:** God is eternal, omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent, holy, just, merciful, loving, faithful, unchanging.
- **Creator:** God created all things ex nihilo (out of nothing) — Gen 1:1, Heb 11:3.
- **Providence:** God sustains and governs all creation (Col 1:17, Acts 17:28), while granting genuine human free will.

**God's Key Attributes for Devotional Writing:**

| Attribute | Scripture | Devotional Application |
|-----------|----------|----------------------|
| Love | 1 John 4:8, 16 | God's love is not just an attribute but His nature |
| Holiness | Isa 6:3, 1 Pet 1:15-16 | The standard for our sanctification |
| Justice | Deut 32:4, Ps 89:14 | God's fairness and righteousness in all dealings |
| Mercy | Ps 103:8, Lam 3:22-23 | God withholds deserved judgment |
| Grace | Eph 2:8-9, Titus 2:11 | God gives what we don't deserve |
| Faithfulness | Deut 7:9, 2 Tim 2:13 | God keeps every promise |
| Sovereignty | Ps 115:3, Dan 4:35 | God is in ultimate control |
| Wisdom | Rom 11:33, Jas 1:5 | God's perfect knowledge applied perfectly |
| Patience | 2 Pet 3:9, Rom 2:4 | God's longsuffering leads to repentance |
| Immutability | Mal 3:6, Jas 1:17 | God does not change |

**How This Shows Up in Devotional Writing:**
- Present God as personal, relational, and knowable — not abstract or distant.
- Balance God's love and mercy with His holiness and justice. Never present a one-dimensional God (all love, no justice — or all wrath, no grace).
- Use Trinitarian language naturally: "God the Father sent His Son... the Spirit indwells believers..."
- Avoid speculative language about the "inner life" of the Trinity. Stick to what Scripture reveals.
- Emphasize God as Creator and Sustainer — grounding identity, purpose, and hope in God's creative and providential work.

### 1.3 Anthropology (The Doctrine of Humanity)

**Key Questions:**
- What does it mean to be made in the image of God (imago Dei)?
- What is the nature of humanity (body, soul, spirit)?
- What is the effect of the Fall on human nature?
- What is original sin? Total depravity?

**Main Positions on Human Nature After the Fall:**
- **Augustinian/Reformed (Total Depravity):** Every part of human nature is corrupted by sin. Humans are unable to choose God apart from efficacious grace. "Total" refers to the scope, not the depth — every faculty is affected, not that humans are as evil as possible.
- **Semi-Pelagian:** Humans can initiate the first step toward God; grace completes the process.
- **Pelagian (HERESY):** Original sin is not transmitted. Humans can be saved by their own efforts without grace.
- **Eastern Orthodox:** Humans inherit the consequences of Adam's sin (mortality, inclination to sin) but not personal guilt for Adam's sin.

**Restoration Movement Position:**
- **Image of God affirmed.** Humans are created in God's image (Gen 1:26-27), giving every person transcendent value, worth, and dignity. The image is marred but not destroyed by sin.
- **The Fall is real.** Adam and Eve's sin introduced death, separation from God, and a universal inclination toward sin (Rom 3:23, 5:12).
- **Rejects Calvinist "Total Depravity"** in the strong Reformed sense. Humans are fallen and sinful, but retain genuine free will and the ability to respond to God's call. People can hear the gospel, believe, and choose to obey — they are not so depraved that they cannot respond without irresistible grace.
- **No inherited guilt from Adam.** Each person is accountable for their own sin (Ezek 18:20). Babies and young children are safe — they have not reached an "age of accountability" where they can knowingly sin. (This is widely held in Churches of Christ, though not universally formalized.)
- **Trichotomy vs. Dichotomy:** Not heavily debated. Most Churches of Christ simply affirm that humans have a physical body and an immaterial soul/spirit that survives physical death.

**How This Shows Up in Devotional Writing:**
- Affirm the reader's intrinsic worth as an image-bearer of God — regardless of their failures, past, or current struggles.
- Be honest about sin without being harsh. Sin is real, universal (Rom 3:23), and serious — but it is not the final word. Grace is.
- Avoid language that implies babies are born guilty or condemned. Children are innocent until they reach accountability.
- Frame the human condition as "broken but not beyond repair" — emphasizing the hope of redemption.
- When discussing temptation, affirm human responsibility (Jas 1:14-15) without reducing humans to puppets or victims.
- Affirm free will: God invites, persuades, calls — but does not coerce.

### 1.4 Christology (The Doctrine of Christ)

*See Section 2 for full treatment.*

**Key Summary for This Locus:**
- Jesus is fully God and fully man (hypostatic union) — two natures, one person. Affirmed at Chalcedon (451 AD).
- Jesus is the only Lord and Savior (Acts 4:12, John 14:6).
- Born of a virgin, lived sinlessly, was crucified for our sins, buried, bodily resurrected on the third day, ascended to the right hand of the Father, and will return.
- His threefold office: Prophet (reveals God), Priest (mediates between God and humanity), King (rules and reigns).

**Restoration Movement Position:** Full affirmation of Chalcedonian Christology. Jesus is divine, not merely a good teacher or moral example. His bodily resurrection is historical fact, not metaphor.

### 1.5 Pneumatology (The Doctrine of the Holy Spirit)

*See Section 3 for full treatment.*

**Key Summary for This Locus:**
- The Holy Spirit is a distinct person of the Trinity — not merely a force or influence.
- The Spirit's work: conviction of sin (John 16:8), regeneration (Titus 3:5), indwelling (1 Cor 6:19), sanctification (2 Thess 2:13), empowerment for service, producing spiritual fruit (Gal 5:22-23).
- The "miraculous gifts" debate (cessationism vs. continuationism) is an area of internal disagreement within the Restoration Movement.

**Restoration Movement Position:** The Spirit is personal and active. Historically cessationist (miraculous gifts ceased with the apostolic age), but the indwelling of the Spirit in every baptized believer is strongly affirmed.

---

### 1.6 Soteriology (The Doctrine of Salvation)

*See Section 4 for full treatment.*

**Key Summary for This Locus:**
- Salvation is by grace through faith — but "through faith" includes an obedient response (repentance, confession, baptism).
- The Restoration Movement order: Hear the gospel → Believe → Repent → Confess Christ → Be baptized (immersion) for the remission of sins → Live faithfully.
- Baptism is not a "work" that earns salvation but a "condition of grace" — the point at which God's grace is applied.
- Rejects both "faith alone" (sola fide in its simplest form) and "works-based salvation."

### 1.7 Ecclesiology (The Doctrine of the Church)

**Key Questions:**
- What is the church? Who belongs to it?
- How should the church be organized and governed?
- What are the ordinances/sacraments?
- What is the church's mission?

**Main Positions on Church Government:**
- **Episcopal:** Bishops oversee multiple congregations in a hierarchy (Catholic, Anglican, Methodist).
- **Presbyterian:** Elders govern in a representative system with higher courts (Presbyterian, Reformed).
- **Congregational:** Each local congregation is autonomous and self-governing (Baptist, Churches of Christ).

**Restoration Movement Position:**
- **The church is the body of baptized believers** — all who have obeyed the gospel by faith, repentance, and baptism. The church is not a denomination but the universal body of Christ.
- **Autonomous congregations.** Each local church is self-governing. There is no denominational headquarters, no convention, no synod, no hierarchy above the local elders.
- **Leadership: Elders (also called shepherds, pastors, overseers, bishops — all referring to the same office) and deacons.**
  - A plurality of elders governs each congregation (1 Tim 3:1-7, Titus 1:5-9).
  - Deacons serve under the elders (1 Tim 3:8-13).
  - Evangelists/ministers preach and teach but do not hold authority over the elders.
- **Worship practices:**
  - **A cappella singing** (no instrumental music) — based on the NT pattern of "speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs" (Eph 5:19, Col 3:16) with no mention of instruments.
  - **Weekly Lord's Supper** — every first day of the week (Acts 20:7). The Lord's Supper commemorates Christ's death, proclaims His return, and calls for self-examination (1 Cor 11:23-29).
  - **Prayer, preaching, Scripture reading, and giving** on the first day of the week.
- **Giving:** Proportional, cheerful, voluntary giving on the first day of the week (1 Cor 16:1-2, 2 Cor 9:7). Not a mandated tithe — the New Testament does not prescribe a percentage. Give "as you have prospered."
- **Role of women:** Women participate fully in worship (singing, prayer, communion, study) but traditionally do not serve as elders, deacons, or preachers in the public assembly. Based on 1 Tim 2:11-12 and 1 Cor 14:34-35. (This is a topic with growing internal discussion.)
- **Church discipline:** Practiced according to Matt 18:15-17 — private confrontation, then witnesses, then the congregation. Goal is always restoration, not punishment. Withdrawal of fellowship is a last resort (1 Cor 5:1-13, 2 Thess 3:6).
- **Name:** Simply "church of Christ" (Rom 16:16) — not a denominational name but a descriptor. The church belongs to Christ.

**How This Shows Up in Devotional Writing:**
- Emphasize the importance of community, fellowship, and belonging to a local body.
- Frame the church as family, not institution. Use warm, relational language.
- When discussing worship, focus on the heart behind the practice — gratitude, remembrance, praise.
- Do NOT write devotionals that attack other denominations or traditions. Focus on positive biblical teaching rather than inter-church polemics.
- The Lord's Supper is a powerful devotional theme — remembrance, gratitude, community, anticipation of Christ's return.
- Emphasize church as "the gathered people of God" not "a building."

### 1.8 Eschatology (The Doctrine of Last Things)

*See Section 5 for full treatment.*

**Key Summary for This Locus:**
- Christ will return bodily, visibly, and personally.
- There will be a resurrection of the dead (both righteous and unrighteous).
- There will be a final judgment.
- Heaven and hell are real, eternal destinations.
- The Restoration Movement is generally amillennial (though there is room for variation).

## 2. Christology in Devotionals

### 2.1 The Person of Christ

**The Hypostatic Union (Council of Chalcedon, 451 AD):**
Jesus Christ is one person with two natures — fully divine and fully human — united without confusion, change, division, or separation. He is not half-God and half-man, nor God pretending to be human, nor a human promoted to divinity.

- **Fully God:** John 1:1-3, 14; Col 2:9; Heb 1:3; Phil 2:6; John 10:30
- **Fully Man:** Born of a woman (Gal 4:4), grew (Luke 2:52), was hungry (Matt 4:2), wept (John 11:35), died (Mark 15:37)
- **One Person:** Not two persons acting in concert, but one unified person with two distinct natures

**Why This Matters for Devotionals:**
- Jesus truly understands human suffering, temptation, and weakness because He experienced it (Heb 4:15).
- Jesus has the power to save because He is God (Heb 7:25).
- The incarnation itself is an act of immeasurable love and humility — God entered our broken world.

**The Kenosis (Philippians 2:5-11):**
The Greek word kenosis means "self-emptying." Christ "emptied Himself" (Phil 2:7) — not of His deity, but of the outward appearance and prerogatives of His deity. He voluntarily set aside the independent exercise of certain divine attributes and took on the form of a servant.

- He did not cease to be God. He chose to live within human limitations.
- The kenosis is an expression of grace, love, and humility — His sacrifice was not for His own advantage but for ours.
- **Devotional gold:** "He humbled himself and became obedient to the point of death — even death on a cross" (Phil 2:8). The "mind of Christ" — humility, obedience, self-sacrifice — is the model for Christian living.

**Errors to Avoid:**
- **Docetism:** Christ only appeared to be human (His body was an illusion). WRONG — He was truly, fully human.
- **Adoptionism:** Jesus was a normal human "adopted" as God's Son at baptism. WRONG — He was God from eternity.
- **Arianism:** Jesus was created by God, a lesser divine being. WRONG — He is eternally God (John 1:1).
- **Apollinarianism:** Christ had a human body but a divine mind/soul. WRONG — He had a fully human mind and soul.

### 2.2 Christ's Threefold Office (Munus Triplex)

**Prophet:**
- Christ reveals God to humanity. "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
- Fulfills Deut 18:15-18: "I will raise up for them a prophet like you."
- He speaks God's truth with absolute authority (Matt 7:29).
- **Devotional angle:** Jesus is not just a teacher of truth — He is the Truth (John 14:6). His words are life.

**Priest:**
- Christ mediates between God and humanity. "There is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Tim 2:5).
- He is both the priest and the sacrifice — the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29).
- His priesthood is after the order of Melchizedek, not Levi — eternal, not temporary (Heb 7).
- He intercedes for us at the right hand of God (Rom 8:34, Heb 7:25).
- **Devotional angle:** We can approach God boldly because we have a high priest who sympathizes with our weaknesses (Heb 4:14-16).

**King:**
- Christ rules and reigns over all creation. "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" (Matt 28:18).
- His kingdom is "not of this world" (John 18:36) — it is a spiritual kingdom, the church (Col 1:13).
- He will return as King of kings and Lord of lords (Rev 19:16).
- **Devotional angle:** Surrendering to Christ's lordship is not oppressive — it is liberating. His yoke is easy, His burden is light (Matt 11:28-30).

### 2.3 Atonement Theories

The cross of Christ is the central event of human history. Multiple theories attempt to explain what happened on the cross. **The Restoration Movement does not formally adopt one theory over others**, but the emphasis on Christ's sacrificial death for the forgiveness of sins aligns most closely with substitutionary and victorious models.

**Primary Atonement Theories:**

| Theory | Core Idea | Key Proponent | Key Scripture | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|-----------|--------------|--------------|----------|----------|
| **Penal Substitution** | Christ bore the punishment we deserved, satisfying God's justice | Calvin, Luther | Isa 53:5-6; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24; Rom 3:25 | Explains how God can be just and justify sinners | Can sound transactional if taught poorly |
| **Christus Victor** | Christ's death and resurrection defeat sin, death, and the devil | Irenaeus, Aulen | Col 2:15; 1 John 3:8; Heb 2:14-15; 1 Cor 15:55-57 | Captures cosmic scope; narrative of victory | Less clear on how forgiveness works mechanically |
| **Ransom** | Christ's death "buys back" humanity from bondage | Origen, early fathers | Mark 10:45; 1 Tim 2:6; 1 Pet 1:18-19 | Emphasizes liberation; strong biblical language | To whom is the ransom paid? |
| **Satisfaction** | Christ's death restores God's honor, violated by sin | Anselm | Rom 3:25-26; Heb 9:22 | Emphasizes God's holiness | Can separate from penal element |
| **Moral Influence** | The cross reveals God's love, inspiring repentance | Abelard | John 3:16; Rom 5:8; 1 John 4:10 | Captures the love dimension | Insufficient alone — does not explain how sin is dealt with |
| **Governmental** | The cross demonstrates that sin has consequences, upholding God's moral law | Grotius | Rom 3:25-26 | Connects to God's justice without strict equivalence | Unclear why Christ specifically must die |
| **Scapegoat** | Christ's death exposes and breaks the cycle of scapegoating violence | Girard | Lev 16; Isa 53; John 11:50 | Fresh pastoral lens | Can minimize divine initiative |

**Recommended Approach for Devotional Writing:**
- **Use a multi-faceted approach.** The cross is too rich for one theory. Draw from multiple angles depending on the devotional's theme:
  - For **guilt and forgiveness** → lean into substitutionary language (He took our place).
  - For **fear and bondage** → lean into Christus Victor language (He defeated the enemy).
  - For **shame and unworthiness** → lean into the love and ransom language (He paid the price; we are bought with a price).
  - For **meaning and transformation** → the cross reveals the depth of God's love and calls us to a new way of living.
- **Always center the cross as real, historical, and effective** — not merely symbolic or illustrative.
- **Avoid reducing the cross** to only one dimension (e.g., only juridical, or only exemplary).

## 3. Pneumatology for Devotionals

### 3.1 The Person of the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is not an impersonal force, power, or energy. He is a distinct person of the Trinity.

**Evidence of Personhood:**
- He has a mind (Rom 8:27), a will (1 Cor 12:11), and emotions (Eph 4:30 — can be grieved).
- He speaks (Acts 13:2), teaches (John 14:26), intercedes (Rom 8:26), testifies (John 15:26).
- He can be lied to (Acts 5:3), resisted (Acts 7:51), and blasphemed (Matt 12:31).

**Evidence of Deity:**
- Called God (Acts 5:3-4), has divine attributes (omniscient — 1 Cor 2:10-11; omnipresent — Ps 139:7-10; eternal — Heb 9:14).
- Involved in creation (Gen 1:2), in the incarnation (Luke 1:35), in resurrection (Rom 8:11).

### 3.2 The Holy Spirit's Work

| Work | Description | Key Scripture | Devotional Application |
|------|------------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Conviction** | Convinces the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment | John 16:8-11 | That restless feeling about sin is the Spirit's work |
| **Regeneration** | The Spirit is the agent of new birth | Titus 3:5; John 3:5 | Being "born again" is not self-improvement — it's spiritual rebirth |
| **Indwelling** | The Spirit lives in every baptized believer | 1 Cor 6:19; Acts 2:38; Rom 8:9 | You are a temple of the Holy Spirit — you are never alone |
| **Sanctification** | The Spirit progressively transforms believers into Christ's likeness | 2 Thess 2:13; Gal 5:16-25 | Spiritual growth is a cooperative work — the Spirit empowers, we yield |
| **Empowerment** | The Spirit gives strength for service and witness | Acts 1:8; Eph 3:16 | God doesn't call the equipped — He equips the called |
| **Guidance** | The Spirit leads believers through Scripture, conscience, and providence | Rom 8:14; Gal 5:18 | "Led by the Spirit" = walking in obedience to God's revealed will |
| **Intercession** | The Spirit prays for us when we can't find words | Rom 8:26-27 | Even in your deepest struggles, the Spirit is advocating for you |
| **Fruit** | The Spirit produces character transformation | Gal 5:22-23 | Love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control |
| **Sealing/Guarantee** | The Spirit is the deposit guaranteeing our inheritance | Eph 1:13-14; 2 Cor 1:22 | The Spirit's presence is God's promise that He will finish what He started |

### 3.3 The Fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23)

This is one of the most devotionally rich topics in all of Scripture. The fruit (singular — one cluster, not nine separate fruits) is the character of Christ produced in believers by the Holy Spirit.

| Fruit | Greek | Meaning | Opposite (Flesh) | Devotional Theme |
|-------|-------|---------|------------------|-----------------|
| Love | agape | Self-sacrificial, unconditional love | Hatred, selfishness | God's nature expressed through us |
| Joy | chara | Deep, settled gladness independent of circumstances | Despair, bitterness | Joy is rooted in who God is, not what's happening |
| Peace | eirene | Wholeness, harmony with God and others | Anxiety, conflict | Peace is not the absence of storms but the presence of Christ |
| Patience | makrothumia | Long-suffering, endurance under provocation | Rage, short temper | God is patient with us; we extend that patience to others |
| Kindness | chrestotes | Moral goodness expressed in action | Cruelty, harshness | Kindness is love made visible |
| Goodness | agathosune | Active benevolence, doing what is right | Wickedness | Goodness sometimes confronts; it's not just niceness |
| Faithfulness | pistis | Reliability, trustworthiness, loyalty | Unfaithfulness, fickleness | God is faithful; His Spirit grows faithfulness in us |
| Gentleness | prautes | Strength under control, meekness | Arrogance, harshness | Gentleness is not weakness — it's power restrained |
| Self-Control | egkrateia | Mastery over impulses and desires | Excess, impulsivity | The Spirit empowers what willpower alone cannot sustain |

### 3.4 The Miraculous Gifts Debate

**This is an area where the Restoration Movement has internal diversity, and devotional writing should handle it with care.**

**Cessationism** (historically dominant in Churches of Christ):
- Miraculous gifts (tongues, prophecy, healing, etc.) ceased with the death of the apostles and the completion of the New Testament canon.
- The sign gifts authenticated the apostles and the gospel message during the foundational period of the church (Heb 2:3-4, 2 Cor 12:12).
- "When the perfect comes, the partial will pass away" (1 Cor 13:10) is interpreted as the completed canon of Scripture.

**Continuationism** (growing minority within the movement, dominant in Pentecostal/Charismatic traditions):
- All gifts of the Spirit remain active until Christ returns.
- The "perfect" in 1 Cor 13:10 refers to Christ's return, not the canon.
- Spiritual gifts are given as the Spirit wills (1 Cor 12:11) — we should not limit God.

**Devotional Writing Guidelines on the Spirit:**
- **DO** write about the Spirit as personal, active, and present in every believer's life.
- **DO** emphasize the Spirit's role in conviction, comfort, guidance, and character transformation (fruit).
- **DO** write about the Spirit empowering believers for service, witness, and endurance.
- **DO** affirm that the Spirit "helps in our weakness" and "intercedes for us" (Rom 8:26).
- **DO NOT** make claims about modern miraculous gifts (tongues, healing, prophecy) — this is divisive territory. Stay neutral.
- **DO NOT** reduce the Spirit to a force or power rather than a person.
- **DO NOT** write that the Spirit "left" or "departed" from a believer as a threat. The Spirit's indwelling is a promise, not a performance contract.
- **SAFE LANGUAGE:** "The Spirit is at work in you," "The Spirit guides us through God's Word," "The Spirit produces fruit in our lives as we yield to Him," "The Spirit empowers us for every good work."

## 4. Soteriology in Depth

### 4.1 The Restoration Movement Order of Salvation (Ordo Salutis)

The Restoration Movement has a distinctive soteriology that differs from both Reformed and Arminian frameworks. Here is the order as taught:

**Step 1: Hear the Gospel**
- Rom 10:17: "Faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."
- The gospel must be proclaimed. No one can respond to what they haven't heard.

**Step 2: Believe / Have Faith**
- John 8:24: "Unless you believe that I am he, you will die in your sins."
- Heb 11:6: "Without faith it is impossible to please God."
- Faith is not mere intellectual assent — it is trust that leads to action (Jas 2:17-26).

**Step 3: Repent**
- Acts 2:38: "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins."
- Acts 17:30: "God now commands all people everywhere to repent."
- Repentance is a change of mind that produces a change of direction — turning away from sin and toward God.

**Step 4: Confess Christ**
- Rom 10:9-10: "If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved."
- Acts 8:37 (in some manuscripts): "I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God."

**Step 5: Be Baptized (Immersion)**
- Acts 2:38: "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins."
- Mark 16:16: "Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved."
- Acts 22:16: "Rise and be baptized and wash away your sins, calling on his name."
- Rom 6:3-4: Baptism is participation in Christ's death, burial, and resurrection.
- Gal 3:27: "All of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ."
- 1 Pet 3:21: "Baptism now saves you — not the removal of dirt from the body, but the pledge of a clear conscience toward God."

**Step 6: Live Faithfully (Sanctification)**
- Phil 2:12: "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling."
- 2 Pet 1:5-10: Add to your faith virtue, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, mutual affection, love.
- Rev 2:10: "Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life."

### 4.2 How This Differs from Reformed and Arminian Views

| Doctrine | Reformed (TULIP) | Arminian | Restoration Movement |
|---------|------------------|----------|---------------------|
| **Depravity** | Total — humans cannot choose God without irresistible grace | Serious — but prevenient grace enables response | Serious — but humans retain genuine free will to respond to the gospel |
| **Election** | Unconditional — God chose specific individuals before creation | Conditional — based on God's foreknowledge of faith | Conditional — God chose a plan (salvation in Christ), not arbitrary individuals. "Whosoever will" (Rev 22:17) |
| **Atonement** | Limited — Christ died only for the elect | Universal — Christ died for all | Universal — Christ died for all (1 John 2:2, 2 Pet 3:9) |
| **Grace** | Irresistible — the elect cannot reject God's call | Resistible — humans can resist grace | Resistible — God calls, but humans must freely respond (Acts 7:51) |
| **Perseverance** | Certain — the elect will persevere; "once saved, always saved" | Conditional — believers can fall away | Conditional — believers can fall away and be lost (Heb 6:4-6, 2 Pet 2:20-22, Gal 5:4). Salvation is secure in Christ, but not unconditional — it requires continued faithfulness |
| **Baptism** | Sign of the covenant; not salvific | Ordinance of obedience; follows salvation | Essential response to grace; the point at which God applies forgiveness through Christ's blood |
| **Faith** | Gift of God given to the elect | Human response to prevenient grace | Human response to the gospel, demonstrated through obedience |

### 4.3 The Baptism Question: Nuances for Devotional Writing

The Restoration Movement's position on baptism is its most distinctive (and most debated) doctrinal emphasis. Here is how to handle it in devotional writing:

**What Baptism IS (in this tradition):**
- An act of faith, not a "work" that earns salvation.
- The point of contact with Christ's blood — where grace is applied (Rom 6:3-4).
- A "condition of grace" — God prescribed it; we obey it; He saves.
- An expression of obedient faith — faith that acts (Jas 2:17).
- Burial with Christ and resurrection to new life (Col 2:12).

**What Baptism is NOT:**
- A magic ritual that saves apart from faith.
- A human work that earns God's favor.
- Merely symbolic (it is symbolic AND effectual).
- Optional for someone who has heard and understood the gospel.

**Devotional Language Guidelines:**
- **USE:** "In baptism, we are united with Christ in His death and resurrection" (Rom 6:3-4). "We were buried with Him in baptism and raised to walk in newness of life."
- **USE:** "Baptism is where our faith meets God's grace."
- **AVOID:** "Baptism saves you all by itself" (sounds mechanical/magical).
- **AVOID:** "If you believe, you're already saved — baptism is just a symbol" (contradicts the tradition's reading of Acts 2:38, Mark 16:16).
- **AVOID:** Making baptism the ONLY topic. The tradition is broader than baptism. Don't be reductive.
- **FRAME IT AS:** An integral part of the whole conversion story — hearing, believing, repenting, confessing, being baptized, living faithfully. Don't isolate any one step.

### 4.4 Sanctification

**Progressive sanctification** is the ongoing work of the Holy Spirit transforming believers into the likeness of Christ. It is not instantaneous perfection but a lifelong journey.

- **Positional sanctification:** At baptism, the believer is "set apart" — declared holy in Christ (1 Cor 6:11).
- **Progressive sanctification:** Daily growth in Christlikeness through the Spirit's work, Scripture, prayer, and community (2 Cor 3:18, Phil 1:6).
- **Final sanctification (Glorification):** At Christ's return, believers are fully transformed (1 John 3:2, Phil 3:20-21).

**Key principles for devotional writing:**
- Sanctification is cooperative — God's Spirit empowers; we yield, obey, and practice (Phil 2:12-13).
- Growth is gradual. There will be setbacks. Grace covers the stumbles.
- The goal is not legalistic perfection but relational maturity — becoming more like Jesus in character, not just behavior.

### 4.5 Assurance and Perseverance

**Restoration Movement position:** Salvation is secure in Christ, but **conditional** upon continued faith and faithfulness. "Once saved, always saved" is rejected.

**Biblical basis for the possibility of falling away:**
- Heb 6:4-6: Those who were enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, shared in the Holy Spirit... and then fell away — cannot be brought back to repentance.
- 2 Pet 2:20-22: Those who knew the Lord and Savior and then turn back — their last state is worse than the first.
- Gal 5:4: "You who are trying to be justified by the law have been alienated from Christ; you have fallen from grace."
- Heb 3:12: "See to it that none of you has a sinful, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God."
- 1 Cor 10:12: "Let anyone who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall."

**Biblical basis for assurance:**
- 1 John 5:13: "I write these things to you who believe... so that you may know you have eternal life."
- Rom 8:38-39: Nothing can separate us from the love of God — but this is addressed to those who are "in Christ Jesus."
- Phil 1:6: "He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion."

**Devotional writing guidelines:**
- **DO** write with confidence and assurance — God is faithful, His promises are sure.
- **DO** encourage perseverance — keep running the race (Heb 12:1).
- **DO NOT** use fear as the primary motivator. Grace motivates more powerfully than fear.
- **DO NOT** suggest that every stumble means loss of salvation. There is a difference between struggling with sin and abandoning Christ.
- **BALANCE:** "God holds you securely — keep holding on to Him."

## 5. Eschatology Primer

### 5.1 The Three Major Millennial Views

| View | Definition | Key Proponents | Interpretation of Rev 20 |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------------------------|
| **Amillennialism** | No literal 1,000-year earthly reign. The "millennium" is the current church age — Christ reigns spiritually now through His church. | Augustine, many Reformers, Churches of Christ | The 1,000 years is symbolic of the entire period between Christ's first and second coming |
| **Premillennialism** | Christ returns BEFORE the millennium. He will physically reign on earth for 1,000 years. | Dispensationalists (Darby, Scofield), many evangelicals | The 1,000 years is a literal future period after Christ's return |
| **Postmillennialism** | Christ returns AFTER the millennium. The gospel will gradually transform the world until Christ returns. | Jonathan Edwards, B.B. Warfield | The 1,000 years represents a golden age of gospel influence before Christ's return |

### 5.2 Interpretive Methods

| Method | Approach | Typical Millennial View |
|--------|----------|----------------------|
| **Futurism** | Most of Revelation prophesies events still in the future | Premillennialism |
| **Preterism** (partial) | Much of Revelation was fulfilled in the destruction of Jerusalem (70 AD) and the fall of Rome. The Second Coming is still future. | Amillennialism or Postmillennialism |
| **Historicism** | Revelation maps the entire history of the church from the first century to the end | Various |
| **Idealism** | Revelation uses symbolic imagery to depict timeless spiritual truths — the battle between good and evil | Amillennialism |

### 5.3 Restoration Movement Position

- **Generally amillennial.** The prevalent view is that Christ is reigning now at the right hand of the Father (Acts 2:33-36) and that the church IS the kingdom (Col 1:13).
- **Originally postmillennial** in the 19th century (optimistic about gospel progress). This shifted toward amillennialism by the World War I era.
- **Anti-dispensationalist.** The Restoration Movement strongly rejects dispensationalist premillennialism (Left Behind-style theology) — particularly the rapture theology, the seven-year tribulation, a rebuilt temple, and a future literal millennial kingdom on earth.
- **The "last days" began at Pentecost** (Acts 2:16-17, quoting Joel 2:28-32). We are living in the last days now.
- **Christ's kingdom is His church**, established on Pentecost (Acts 2). It is not a future political kingdom.

### 5.4 What IS Agreed Upon (Non-Negotiables)

Regardless of millennial view, these are non-negotiable:
1. **Christ will return** — personally, visibly, bodily (Acts 1:11, 1 Thess 4:16-17).
2. **The dead will be raised** — both righteous and unrighteous (John 5:28-29, Acts 24:15).
3. **There will be a final judgment** — everyone will give account (2 Cor 5:10, Matt 25:31-46, Rev 20:11-15).
4. **Heaven is real** — eternal life with God (John 14:1-3, Rev 21-22).
5. **Hell is real** — eternal separation from God (Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Rev 20:15).

### 5.5 Intermediate State

**Restoration Movement view:** After death and before the final resurrection/judgment, souls exist in an intermediate state (Hades).
- **The righteous** are in a state of comfort/paradise (Luke 16:22, Luke 23:43).
- **The unrighteous** are in a state of torment (Luke 16:23-24).
- This is NOT the final heaven or hell — it is the waiting state before the resurrection and judgment.
- **Purgatory is rejected.** There is no scriptural basis for a purification after death.

### 5.6 Devotional Writing Guidelines for Eschatology

- **DO** write about hope, the return of Christ, the new creation, and the promise of eternity with God. These are powerful devotional themes.
- **DO** write about heaven as real, beautiful, and worth longing for — "our citizenship is in heaven" (Phil 3:20).
- **DO** use the language of "already / not yet" — the kingdom is here now AND not yet fully consummated.
- **DO** emphasize readiness and faithfulness — "Be faithful unto death" (Rev 2:10).
- **DO NOT** speculate about dates, timelines, or specific prophetic fulfillments.
- **DO NOT** promote rapture theology, "Left Behind" scenarios, or detailed charts of end-time events.
- **DO NOT** use eschatology to generate fear. The Second Coming is a source of HOPE for believers (Titus 2:13).
- **DO NOT** take a position on amillennialism vs. premillennialism vs. postmillennialism in devotional content. Focus on what all Christians agree on: Christ is coming, we should be ready, the best is yet to come.
- **SAFE LANGUAGE:** "One day, Christ will return and make all things new." "We live with hope because we know how the story ends." "God will wipe every tear from their eyes" (Rev 21:4).

## 6. Biblical Theology vs. Systematic Theology

### 6.1 Definitions

**Systematic Theology** arranges biblical teachings into logical categories (God, Christ, salvation, church, etc.) and seeks to state what the WHOLE Bible teaches on each topic. It is topical and synthetic.

**Biblical Theology** traces the progressive revelation of God through the narrative arc of Scripture. It follows the storyline of the Bible and asks: "How does this passage fit into the unfolding story?" It is narrative and historical.

### 6.2 The Grand Narrative Arc of Scripture

The Bible tells ONE story in FOUR movements:

**1. CREATION (Genesis 1-2)**
- God creates a good world and places humans in it as His image-bearers.
- Everything is "very good" (Gen 1:31).
- Humans are given purpose, relationship, and stewardship.
- **Theological themes:** God's goodness, human dignity, purpose, beauty, order, rest.

**2. FALL (Genesis 3)**
- Humans rebel against God, choosing autonomy over trust.
- Sin enters the world, bringing death, broken relationships, shame, and suffering.
- All of creation is affected (Rom 8:20-22).
- **Theological themes:** Sin, consequences, separation, shame, the origin of suffering.

**3. REDEMPTION (Genesis 12 → Revelation 20)**
- God launches a rescue operation. He calls Abraham, forms Israel, sends prophets, and ultimately sends His Son.
- The entire Old Testament is the long setup — promises, types, shadows, prophecies pointing to Christ.
- Jesus' life, death, and resurrection are the climax of the story.
- The church is the community of the redeemed, continuing Christ's mission.
- **Theological themes:** Grace, covenant, sacrifice, promise, fulfillment, the cross, resurrection, the church.

**4. RESTORATION / NEW CREATION (Revelation 21-22)**
- God will make all things new. Heaven and earth are renewed. Death, sin, and suffering are eliminated.
- "Behold, I am making all things new" (Rev 21:5).
- The story ends not with escape FROM creation but with the renewal OF creation — a new heaven and a new earth.
- **Theological themes:** Hope, consummation, justice, reunion, wholeness, eternal life.

### 6.3 Key Biblical-Theological Themes That Run Through the Whole Story

| Theme | Creation | Fall | Redemption | Restoration |
|-------|----------|------|-----------|------------|
| **God's Presence** | God walks with humanity in Eden | Humanity is exiled from God's presence | The tabernacle/temple; Jesus "tabernacles" among us (John 1:14); the Spirit indwells | God dwells with humanity forever (Rev 21:3) |
| **Temple** | Eden is the first "temple" — God's dwelling | The temple is defiled, eventually destroyed | Jesus is the true temple (John 2:19-21); the church is God's temple (1 Cor 3:16) | No temple needed — God Himself is the temple (Rev 21:22) |
| **Covenant** | Implicit covenant with Adam | Covenant broken | Covenants with Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, the New Covenant in Christ's blood | Covenant fully realized |
| **Kingdom** | God reigns over all creation | Human kingdoms rebel against God's rule | Christ establishes His kingdom (the church) | "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord" (Rev 11:15) |
| **Image of God** | Humanity created in God's image | Image marred by sin | Christ is the perfect image of God (Col 1:15); we are being renewed in that image (2 Cor 3:18) | Fully restored image (1 John 3:2) |

### 6.4 How to Draw from Both in Devotional Writing

**From Systematic Theology:**
- Gives clear doctrinal guardrails — what we affirm and what we reject.
- Provides the vocabulary: justification, sanctification, atonement, Trinity, etc.
- Ensures theological consistency across devotionals.

**From Biblical Theology:**
- Provides the STORY — and humans are wired for story.
- Connects any single passage to the bigger picture.
- Makes the Old Testament accessible and relevant.
- Shows how God's plan unfolds progressively — building anticipation and fulfillment.

**Best Practice for Devotionals:**
- **Start with the text in its narrative context** (biblical theology) — What is happening in the story? Who is involved? What has God been doing?
- **Draw out the theological principle** (systematic theology) — What does this teach us about God, humanity, sin, grace, etc.?
- **Apply it to the reader's life** — How does this truth change how you think, feel, or live today?

**Example:** A devotional on Isaiah 53
- *Biblical theology lens:* This is the Suffering Servant prophecy, the culmination of Isaiah's servant songs, pointing forward to the cross 700 years before it happened. It shows that God's plan of redemption was not an afterthought.
- *Systematic theology lens:* This passage teaches substitutionary atonement — "He was pierced for our transgressions" (v. 5). It addresses Christology (the Messiah's suffering) and soteriology (how we are healed/saved).
- *Devotional application:* "Before you were born, before you sinned, before you even knew you needed rescuing — God had already written the rescue plan. You are not an afterthought. You are loved on purpose."

## 7. Top 50 Doctrinal Topics for Devotionals

Below are the 50 doctrinal topics most likely to surface in daily devotional writing, with the Restoration Movement position and key Scriptures for each.

### Core Salvation & Grace

**1. Grace**
- God's unmerited favor — the foundation of salvation (Eph 2:8-9, Titus 2:11).
- Grace is not permission to sin (Rom 6:1-2); it is the power to live differently.
- RM Position: Grace is the source of salvation, but it is accessed through an obedient faith response.

**2. Faith**
- Trust in God and His promises, demonstrated through obedience (Heb 11:1, Jas 2:17-26).
- Faith is not just intellectual assent — it is active trust that changes behavior.
- RM Position: Faith is essential but not sufficient alone — it must be expressed through repentance, confession, and baptism.

**3. Repentance**
- A change of mind that leads to a change of direction — turning from sin to God (Acts 17:30, 2 Cor 7:10).
- Godly sorrow leads to repentance; worldly sorrow leads to death.
- RM Position: Repentance is commanded by God for all people and is a necessary step before baptism.

**4. Baptism**
- Immersion in water for the forgiveness of sins (Acts 2:38, Rom 6:3-4, Gal 3:27, 1 Pet 3:21).
- RM Position: Baptism is the point at which the penitent believer receives forgiveness and the gift of the Holy Spirit. It is not a "work" but an act of obedient faith.

**5. Salvation**
- Deliverance from sin and its consequences through Christ (Rom 1:16, Acts 4:12, 2 Tim 1:9).
- Past tense (we have been saved — justified), present tense (we are being saved — sanctified), future tense (we will be saved — glorified).
- RM Position: Salvation is by grace through faith, expressed in obedience (hearing, believing, repenting, confessing, being baptized, living faithfully).

**6. Justification**
- Being declared righteous before God — not because of our works but because of Christ's sacrifice (Rom 3:24, 5:1).
- RM Position: Justification occurs when the believer is baptized into Christ and receives forgiveness.

**7. Forgiveness**
- God forgives our sins through Christ's blood (Eph 1:7, 1 John 1:9, Col 1:13-14).
- We are also called to forgive others (Matt 6:14-15, Eph 4:32, Col 3:13).
- RM Position: God's forgiveness is accessed through obedient faith (including baptism). Christians must also practice ongoing forgiveness.

**8. Atonement**
- Christ's death as the provision for our sin (Rom 3:25, 1 John 2:2, Heb 9:22).
- See Section 2.3 for detailed treatment.

### Character of God

**9. God's Love**
- God IS love (1 John 4:8). His love is unconditional, sacrificial, and initiating (Rom 5:8, John 3:16).
- RM Position: God's love motivates His plan of salvation and should motivate our response of obedience.

**10. God's Holiness**
- God is set apart, pure, and morally perfect (Isa 6:3, 1 Pet 1:15-16, Rev 4:8).
- His holiness demands justice for sin — which makes grace all the more remarkable.

**11. God's Sovereignty**
- God is in ultimate control of all things (Ps 115:3, Dan 4:35, Rom 8:28).
- RM Position: God is sovereign but exercises sovereignty in a way that respects human free will. He is not a puppet-master but a loving Father who invites free response.

**12. God's Faithfulness**
- God keeps every promise (Deut 7:9, 2 Tim 2:13, Lam 3:22-23).
- "Great is Thy faithfulness" — His mercies are new every morning.

**13. God's Justice**
- God is perfectly fair and righteous (Ps 89:14, Isa 30:18, Deut 32:4).
- His justice is not opposed to His mercy — both meet at the cross.

**14. God's Mercy**
- God withholds the punishment we deserve (Ps 103:8-12, Eph 2:4-5, Titus 3:5).
- Mercy is not earned; it is given. "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (Hos 6:6, Matt 9:13).

### Christian Living

**15. Prayer**
- Communication with God — praise, confession, thanksgiving, petition (Phil 4:6-7, 1 Thess 5:17, Matt 6:5-13).
- RM Position: Prayer is addressed to the Father, through the Son, empowered by the Spirit. Prayer is not a tool to manipulate God but a relationship of trust and dependence.

**16. Worship**
- Whole-life devotion to God, expressed both corporately and individually (Rom 12:1, John 4:23-24).
- RM Position: Corporate worship includes a cappella singing, prayer, preaching, Lord's Supper, and giving. But worship is bigger than Sunday — it's a daily posture.

**17. The Lord's Supper / Communion**
- Remembrance of Christ's body and blood, observed every first day of the week (1 Cor 11:23-29, Acts 20:7).
- RM Position: Weekly observance; open to all baptized believers. It is a time of remembrance, proclamation, and self-examination.

**18. Scripture / Bible Study**
- The Bible is God's inspired, authoritative, and sufficient Word (2 Tim 3:16-17, Ps 119:105, Heb 4:12).
- RM Position: The Bible is our only rule of faith and practice. Daily reading and study are essential for spiritual growth.

**19. Obedience**
- Loving God is demonstrated through obedience (John 14:15, 1 John 5:3, Jas 1:22).
- RM Position: Obedience is not legalism — it is the natural response of faith. "Faith without works is dead" (Jas 2:26).

**20. Discipleship**
- Following Jesus as a whole-life commitment — learning, growing, serving, sacrificing (Luke 9:23, Matt 28:19-20).
- RM Position: Active obedience is expected, not passive belief. Disciples are learners who put Jesus' teachings into practice.

**21. Sanctification / Holiness**
- The process of becoming more like Christ (2 Cor 3:18, 1 Thess 4:3, Heb 12:14).
- See Section 4.4 for detailed treatment.

**22. The Church / Community**
- The body of Christ; the family of God; a community of baptized believers (Eph 1:22-23, 1 Cor 12:12-27, Heb 10:24-25).
- RM Position: The church is essential — "lone ranger Christianity" is not biblical. Every believer needs a local body.

**23. Evangelism / Mission**
- Sharing the gospel with the world (Matt 28:18-20, Acts 1:8, Rom 10:14-15).
- RM Position: Every Christian is called to share their faith. The Great Commission is for all believers, not just preachers.

**24. Service / Good Works**
- Created in Christ Jesus for good works (Eph 2:10, Gal 6:9-10, Matt 25:35-40).
- Good works are the FRUIT of salvation, not the ROOT of it.

**25. Stewardship / Generosity**
- Everything belongs to God; we are managers/stewards (Ps 24:1, 1 Cor 4:2, 2 Cor 9:7).
- RM Position: Proportional, cheerful giving (1 Cor 16:1-2). The NT does not mandate a tithe — give as you have prospered, joyfully and deliberately.

### Suffering & Struggle

**26. Suffering / Trials**
- Suffering is real and painful but purposeful (Rom 5:3-5, Jas 1:2-4, 1 Pet 1:6-7, 2 Cor 4:17).
- God does not cause suffering but uses it for our growth and His glory.
- RM Position: Suffering is not a sign of God's absence or displeasure. It is part of life in a fallen world, and God is present in it.

**27. Theodicy / Problem of Evil**
- Why does God allow suffering? Because free will is real, and a world with genuine freedom includes the possibility of evil.
- God is not the author of evil. Satan, human sin, and the brokenness of creation are the sources.
- RM Position: "Time and chance" play a role (Eccl 9:11). Not everything happens "for a reason" — but God can bring good from all things (Rom 8:28).

**28. Temptation**
- Temptation comes from our own desires, not from God (Jas 1:13-15). God provides a way of escape (1 Cor 10:13).
- Jesus was tempted and overcame — He understands our struggle (Heb 4:15).

**29. Spiritual Warfare**
- There is a real spiritual battle (Eph 6:10-18). Our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces.
- The armor of God: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the word of God, prayer.
- RM Position: Spiritual warfare is real but should be understood through Scripture, not speculation. The primary battle is against sin in our own hearts, empowered by Satan's influence. Avoid hyper-focus on demons or attributing every problem to demonic activity.

**30. Anxiety / Fear / Mental Health**
- "Do not be anxious about anything" (Phil 4:6-7). "Fear not" appears frequently in Scripture.
- Depression is NOT a sin. Biblical figures like David, Elijah, and Job experienced deep despair.
- RM Position: Faith and professional help can work together. Prayer, Scripture, community, AND counseling/medicine are all valid. The Spirit works through means. NEVER suggest that mental health struggles are simply a "lack of faith."

### Theological Concepts

**31. The Trinity**
- One God, three persons: Father, Son, Holy Spirit (Matt 28:19, 2 Cor 13:14, John 14-16).
- RM Position: Affirmed as biblical truth, even if the term itself is extrabiblical. Use Trinitarian language naturally.

**32. The Incarnation**
- God became flesh in Jesus (John 1:14, Phil 2:5-8, Heb 2:14-17).
- The most radical act of love in history — the Creator entered creation.

**33. The Resurrection**
- The bodily resurrection of Jesus is the foundation of the Christian faith (1 Cor 15:14-17, Rom 1:4, Acts 2:24).
- Without the resurrection, our faith is in vain.
- RM Position: Historical, physical, bodily resurrection — not metaphor or spiritual experience.

**34. The Second Coming**
- Christ will return visibly, bodily, and personally (Acts 1:11, 1 Thess 4:16-17, Rev 22:20).
- RM Position: Certain, but unpredictable. Live in readiness and hope, not fear and speculation.

**35. Heaven**
- Eternal life in the presence of God — "no more death or mourning or crying or pain" (Rev 21:4, John 14:1-3, Phil 3:20).
- Heaven is not "floating on clouds" — it is the new creation, fully restored.

**36. Hell**
- Eternal separation from God (Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Rev 20:15).
- RM Position: Hell is real and eternal. Not annihilation, not purgatory, not universalism. But devotionals should present hell as a warning that motivates urgency about salvation, not as a weapon to terrorize people.

**37. Judgment**
- All will stand before God (2 Cor 5:10, Matt 25:31-46, Heb 9:27, Rev 20:11-15).
- Judgment is based on deeds/faithfulness (Matt 16:27, Rev 22:12) — not contradicting grace, because genuine faith produces obedient living.

**38. Predestination / Free Will**
- RM Position: God predestined a PLAN (salvation in Christ), not individual people to be saved or lost. "Whosoever will may come" (Rev 22:17). Election is corporate (God chose the church) and conditional (based on faith).
- Human free will is real. God invites, He does not compel.
- Both God's sovereignty and human responsibility are biblical truths held in tension.
- **Devotional language:** "God chose you — and He's asking you to choose Him back." "You are not a puppet; you are a partner in God's purposes."

**39. The New Covenant**
- Christ established a new covenant through His blood (Luke 22:20, Heb 8:6-13, Jer 31:31-34).
- Christians are under the new covenant, not the old (Mosaic) law.
- RM Position: The Old Testament is inspired Scripture for learning and wisdom (Rom 15:4, 2 Tim 3:16), but Christians are not bound by Mosaic law (sacrifices, festivals, dietary laws). The New Testament is the law of Christ for the church.

**40. The Kingdom of God**
- The kingdom is both present (Christ reigns now through His church — Col 1:13) and future (fully consummated at His return).
- RM Position: The church IS the kingdom, established on Pentecost (Acts 2). It is not a future political entity.

### Relationships & Ethics

**41. Marriage**
- God-designed covenant between one man and one woman for life (Gen 2:24, Matt 19:4-6, Eph 5:22-33).
- RM Position: Marriage is permanent. Divorce is only permissible on the grounds of sexual immorality (Matt 19:9), and only the innocent party may remarry. This is a topic with some internal variation in interpretation.

**42. Forgiveness of Others**
- We forgive as we have been forgiven (Matt 6:14-15, Eph 4:32, Col 3:13).
- Unforgiveness is a prison — forgiveness sets the forgiver free.

**43. Unity Among Believers**
- Jesus prayed for unity (John 17:20-21). Paul commanded it (Eph 4:1-6, 1 Cor 1:10).
- RM Position: Unity is not uniformity. The Restoration Movement began as a unity movement — "Christians only, but not the only Christians." Seek the common ground of Scripture.

**44. Love for Neighbor / Compassion**
- The second greatest commandment (Matt 22:39). The Good Samaritan is the model (Luke 10:25-37).
- Love is action, not just feeling (1 John 3:18, Jas 2:15-17).

**45. Humility**
- Christ is the ultimate model of humility (Phil 2:3-8). Humility is not weakness — it is strength surrendered.
- "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (Jas 4:6, 1 Pet 5:5-6).

### Contested / Sensitive Topics

**46. Spiritual Gifts**
- The Spirit gives gifts for the building up of the church (1 Cor 12, Rom 12:3-8, Eph 4:11-13).
- RM Position: All Christians have gifts for service. The question of miraculous gifts (tongues, healing) is debated — devotionals should focus on serving gifts and the fruit of the Spirit rather than the miraculous gifts debate.

**47. Prosperity Gospel (REJECT)**
- The false teaching that God rewards faith with material wealth and physical health.
- RM Position: This is a dangerous distortion of the gospel. Jesus promised tribulation (John 16:33), not prosperity. Paul knew both abundance and need (Phil 4:12). Devotionals must NEVER suggest that poverty or illness indicates lack of faith.

**48. Works and Grace (The Balance)**
- Saved by grace (Eph 2:8-9), created for good works (Eph 2:10). Works are the evidence of faith, not the basis of salvation.
- RM Position: Faith and works are not opposed — faith works (Gal 5:6). James 2:24: "A person is justified by works and not by faith alone." But this is a "justified" in the sense of "shown to be genuine," not "earned."

**49. Suffering and God's Will**
- Not all suffering is God's will, but God works in all things (Rom 8:28).
- Suffering can produce perseverance, character, and hope (Rom 5:3-5).
- NEVER say "God caused this" about a person's suffering. Say "God is with you in this."

**50. Identity in Christ**
- In Christ: chosen (Eph 1:4), adopted (Eph 1:5), forgiven (Eph 1:7), sealed (Eph 1:13), new creation (2 Cor 5:17), God's workmanship (Eph 2:10), temple of the Spirit (1 Cor 6:19).
- Identity comes from who God says you are, not from performance, appearance, or approval.

## 8. Red Flags / Avoid List

### 8.1 Theological Errors to NEVER Produce

The AI devotional engine must NEVER generate content that teaches, endorses, or implicitly supports any of the following:

#### Trinitarian Heresies

| Heresy | Error | Orthodox Response |
|--------|-------|------------------|
| **Modalism / Sabellianism** | God is one person in three modes (masks) — Father, Son, and Spirit are not distinct persons | God is ONE God in THREE distinct, co-eternal persons. The Father is not the Son; the Son is not the Spirit. |
| **Arianism** | Jesus is a created being — divine, but not fully God | Jesus is eternally God, of the same substance (homoousios) as the Father (John 1:1, Col 2:9) |
| **Tritheism** | There are three separate Gods | There is ONE God (Deut 6:4) who exists eternally as three persons |
| **Oneness Pentecostalism** | Modern form of Modalism — "Jesus Only" theology; denies distinct persons of Trinity; re-baptizes in "Jesus' name only" | The baptismal formula is "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" (Matt 28:19) — three distinct persons |

#### Christological Heresies

| Heresy | Error | Orthodox Response |
|--------|-------|------------------|
| **Docetism** | Jesus only APPEARED to be human; His body was an illusion | Jesus was fully, truly human (1 John 4:2, Heb 2:14) |
| **Adoptionism** | Jesus was a normal man "adopted" as Son of God at baptism | Jesus was God's Son from eternity (John 1:1, 8:58) |
| **Apollinarianism** | Jesus had a human body but divine mind | Jesus was fully human in body AND mind/soul (Luke 2:52, Matt 26:38) |
| **Nestorianism** | Jesus was two persons (divine and human) loosely connected | Jesus is ONE person with two natures (hypostatic union) |
| **Eutychianism** | Christ's human nature was absorbed into His divine nature | Two natures remain distinct — without confusion, change, division, or separation |

#### Soteriological Errors

| Error | Description | Why It's Wrong |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Prosperity Gospel** | God rewards faith with wealth and health; poverty/illness = lack of faith | Jesus was poor (2 Cor 8:9); Paul had a thorn (2 Cor 12:7-10); the apostles suffered (2 Cor 11:24-28). God never promised material wealth to the faithful. |
| **Universalism** | Everyone will eventually be saved; hell is empty or temporary | Scripture clearly teaches that some will be eternally separated from God (Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Rev 20:15) |
| **Pelagianism** | Humans can save themselves by good works without grace | "By grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves" (Eph 2:8). We cannot earn salvation. |
| **Antinomianism** | Since we're saved by grace, moral law doesn't apply; sin freely | "Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? By no means!" (Rom 6:1-2) |
| **Legalism** | Salvation is earned by strict rule-keeping | Gal 2:16: "A person is not justified by the works of the law." Legalism trusts in self-righteousness rather than Christ's righteousness. |
| **"Once Saved, Always Saved" (Unconditional Eternal Security)** | A saved person can never lose salvation regardless of behavior | Heb 6:4-6, 2 Pet 2:20-22, Gal 5:4 teach the possibility of falling away. (Note: RM rejects this, but devotionals should not attack OSAS directly — instead, positively emphasize the call to persevere.) |
| **Hyper-Grace** | God's grace covers everything automatically; no need for repentance, obedience, or growth | Grace teaches us to say "no" to ungodliness (Titus 2:11-12). Grace empowers transformation, not passivity. |

#### Eschatological Errors

| Error | Description | Why It's Wrong |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Date-Setting** | Predicting when Christ will return | "No one knows the day or hour" (Matt 24:36). Every date-setter has been wrong. |
| **Rapture Theology** | Secret rapture where Christians are taken before a tribulation period | Not found in Scripture as a separate event from the Second Coming. The Restoration Movement rejects this. |
| **Soul Sleep** | The dead are unconscious until the resurrection | Luke 16:19-31, Luke 23:43, Phil 1:23 indicate conscious existence after death. |

#### Other Theological Errors

| Error | Description | Why It's Wrong |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Gnosticism** | Material world is evil; secret knowledge is needed for salvation | God created the material world "good" (Gen 1:31). Salvation is by grace through faith, not secret knowledge. |
| **Deism** | God created the world but is now absent/uninvolved | God is actively involved in sustaining creation and in the lives of His people (Col 1:17, Matt 28:20) |
| **Open Theism** | God does not know the future | God is omniscient (Ps 139:4, Isa 46:10) |
| **Word of Faith / "Name It and Claim It"** | Spoken words have creative power; you can "decree" things into existence | Only God speaks things into existence. Prayer is requesting, not commanding. |
| **Purgatory** | A place of purification after death before heaven | No scriptural basis. Christ's sacrifice is sufficient (Heb 10:10-14). |
| **Praying to Saints/Mary** | Departed saints can hear and intercede for us | There is one mediator — Christ (1 Tim 2:5). Prayer is directed to the Father, through the Son. |
| **Baptism of the Dead / Proxy Baptism** | Living people can be baptized on behalf of the dead | Heb 9:27: "It is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment." There is no second chance after death. |

### 8.2 Language and Tone to Avoid

**Never use:**
- "God won't give you more than you can handle" — This misquotes 1 Cor 10:13 (about temptation, not suffering) and dismisses real pain.
- "Everything happens for a reason" — Implies God causes evil. Say instead: "God can bring good from even the worst circumstances."
- "Just have more faith and you'll be healed/blessed" — Prosperity gospel territory.
- "God helps those who help themselves" — Not in the Bible. It's from Benjamin Franklin.
- "When God closes a door, He opens a window" — Not biblical. Trite and dismissive of real suffering.
- "Just pray about it" as a dismissal of real problems — Prayer is vital, but so is action, community support, and professional help.
- "You're being punished for your sin" when someone is suffering — This is Job's friends' theology. Jesus rejected it (John 9:1-3).
- "They're in a better place" as the primary response to grief — While true for believers, this dismisses the real pain of loss. Grieve first (Rom 12:15).

**Never adopt a tone that is:**
- Judgmental or condemning toward the reader
- Manipulative (using fear or guilt to coerce behavior)
- Triumphalistic (ignoring the reality of suffering and struggle)
- Dismissive of doubt or honest questions
- Sexist, racist, or demeaning toward any group
- Politically partisan (the gospel is not a political platform)

### 8.3 Sensitive Topics — Handle with Extra Care

These topics require nuance and should be handled with pastoral sensitivity, not dogmatic pronouncements:

1. **Divorce and remarriage** — Painful, complicated. Show compassion. Affirm the ideal without condemning the broken.
2. **LGBTQ+ issues** — The Restoration Movement holds to traditional sexual ethics (marriage = one man, one woman). In devotional content, focus on God's design for sexuality and relationships positively rather than targeting or condemning individuals. Lead with love and the call to follow Christ.
3. **Women's roles** — Affirm women's dignity, giftedness, and value. Avoid language that reduces women or dismisses their contributions.
4. **Mental health** — Validate struggle. Never suggest depression/anxiety is "just" a spiritual problem.
5. **Suicide** — Handle with extreme care. Affirm God's love, encourage reaching out for help. NEVER suggest suicide is "the unforgivable sin."
6. **Racial justice** — The gospel breaks down barriers (Gal 3:28). Affirm the dignity of every person as an image-bearer.
7. **Politics** — The gospel transcends political parties. Devotionals should be apolitical.
8. **Other religions** — Affirm Christ as the only way (John 14:6) without being hateful or derogatory toward people of other faiths.
9. **Wealth and poverty** — Challenge materialism without shaming wealth. Advocate generosity without promoting poverty as virtue.
10. **Denominational differences** — Teach truth positively. Never attack other Christian traditions by name.

## Appendix A: Quick Reference — Doctrinal Guardrails for AI

### ALWAYS Affirm:
- [ ] One God in three persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)
- [ ] Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man
- [ ] Jesus is the ONLY way to salvation (Acts 4:12, John 14:6)
- [ ] The Bible is the inspired, authoritative, sufficient Word of God
- [ ] Salvation is by grace through faith, expressed in obedient response (including baptism)
- [ ] The bodily resurrection of Jesus is historical fact
- [ ] Christ will return personally, visibly, bodily
- [ ] Heaven and hell are real, eternal destinations
- [ ] The church is the body of Christ — community is essential
- [ ] The Holy Spirit indwells every baptized believer
- [ ] Christians are called to active discipleship, not passive belief
- [ ] Every human being is made in God's image and has inherent dignity
- [ ] Forgiveness is available to all through Christ

### NEVER Teach:
- [ ] Prosperity gospel (wealth = God's favor; illness = lack of faith)
- [ ] Universalism (everyone is saved regardless)
- [ ] Modalism/Oneness theology (God is one person in three modes)
- [ ] Arianism (Jesus is less than fully God)
- [ ] Salvation by works alone (earning God's favor through performance)
- [ ] Salvation by faith alone with no response required (no repentance, no baptism)
- [ ] "Once saved, always saved" in its unconditional form
- [ ] Date-setting for Christ's return
- [ ] Rapture theology (secret rapture before tribulation)
- [ ] That suffering is always punishment for sin
- [ ] That mental illness is merely a spiritual problem
- [ ] Word of Faith / "name it and claim it" theology
- [ ] Prayers to saints or Mary
- [ ] Purgatory
- [ ] Open theism (God doesn't know the future)
- [ ] Deism (God is uninvolved in the world)

### When In Doubt, Default To:
- Scripture over tradition
- Compassion over condemnation
- Hope over fear
- Clarity over complexity
- Jesus over theology
- Love over legalism
- Humility over certainty on debatable matters
