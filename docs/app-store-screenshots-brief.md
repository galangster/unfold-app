# Unfold App — App Store Screenshot Redesign
## Creative Brief for Designer

**Prepared:** March 23, 2026
**Version:** 1.0
**Audience:** Visual designer creating App Store screenshots for iPhone 6.9" display (iPhone 17 Pro)

---

## 1. The Problem We Are Solving

The current screenshots do three things wrong:

1. They show generic UI that looks like every other Bible app
2. The headlines describe features instead of outcomes
3. They completely hide Unfold's single biggest differentiator: the adaptive loop

Most people tap "Get" or keep scrolling within 3 seconds of landing on the App Store page. Screenshots 1 and 2 carry the entire conversion burden. Everything after that is for the already-interested person who wants to be convinced. This brief optimizes for both audiences.

---

## 2. Brand Positioning

### The One-Sentence Position
Unfold is the most personal Bible study in the world — a complete spiritual ecosystem for your walk with God.

### What Makes This Different From Every Competitor
Every other app in this category — YouVersion, Bible Recap, First5, Lectio 365 — delivers the same content to everyone. The same Psalm 23 devotional goes to the new believer and the seminary student, the person in grief and the person celebrating a new job.

Unfold knows the difference. Because it asked. Because you told it. Because yesterday's midday check-in and this morning's journal entry changed what it wrote today.

This is not a positioning claim. It is a technical fact. And it's not just a devotional reader — it's the complete package: personalized devotionals, full Bible reader, AI companion, SOAP journaling, prayer tracking, audio narration, 32 study methods, and daily check-ins that shape tomorrow's content. No other app comes close to this breadth, and none of them personalize any of it.

Sell the ecosystem. Sell the premium.

### Key Message Hierarchy

**Level 1 — The Hook (must appear in screenshot 1)**
"The most personal Bible study in the world."

**Level 2 — The Mechanism (screenshots 2–3)**
You share your story. Unfold generates a custom series. Your daily check-ins and reflections shape tomorrow's content. The app literally cannot show you what it shows someone else.

**Level 3 — The Feature Set (screenshots 4–6)**
32 Bible study methods. Full Bible reader. AI companion for questions. SOAP journaling. Audio narration. Streak tracking. PDF export.

**Level 4 — The Price Point (paywall context)**
$8.99/month or $59.99/year. A meaningful investment in your spiritual growth — still less than one dinner out.

### Tone
Not reverent. Not clinical. Warm, direct, and quietly confident — the same voice the app uses. The person who's 5 years further along in faith handing you something that worked for them.

Avoid: "Transform your faith journey." "Experience Scripture like never before." "Powerful AI-powered devotionals."

Aim for: Copy that sounds like a person said it, not an algorithm wrote it.

## 3. Visual Identity Reference

All screenshot frames must use Unfold's established design language. Do not deviate.

### Color Palette

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Background | `#0A0A0A` | Base of all screenshot frames |
| Pure Black | `#000000` | Device bezel, deep sections |
| Elevated Surface | `#141210` | Cards, sheets, overlays |
| Primary Text | `#F5F0EB` | Headlines, body copy |
| Muted Text | `rgba(245, 240, 235, 0.6)` | Subheads, secondary labels |
| Subtle Text | `rgba(245, 240, 235, 0.4)` | Tertiary, hints |
| Signature Accent (Gold) | `#C8A55C` | CTAs, active states, progress, ember particles |
| Border / Divider | `rgba(245, 240, 235, 0.08)` | Subtle card edges |

The gold (`#C8A55C`) is the brand's heartbeat. It appears on the streak flame, the progress bar fill, the active tab dot, the ember particle field. Use it sparingly and it signals quality. Overuse it and it reads as garish.

The warm off-white text (`#F5F0EB`) against near-black backgrounds gives the app a candlelit quality — not harsh white, not cool gray. This distinction matters in screenshots.

### Typography

| Role | Typeface | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Display / Headlines | Instrument Serif (Regular & Italic) | Large overlay headlines in screenshots |
| Body / UI | Inter | Subheads, labels, supporting copy |

The Instrument Serif display font is Unfold's most recognizable visual element. It reads as thoughtful, literary, and grounded — not flashy. Use it for the large "hero" headline in each screenshot overlay. The italic variant adds emphasis without weight.

**Recommended headline sizing for screenshot overlays:**
- Primary headline: 48–60pt Instrument Serif
- Subhead: 14–16pt Inter Medium, tracked slightly (+10–20)
- Feature callout labels: 12pt Inter SemiBold, uppercase, +40 tracking

### Don't Use
- Rounded display fonts (Poppins, Nunito, etc.) — wrong energy
- Any color outside the palette above for the headline overlays
- Full-bleed stock photography of open Bibles, candles, or fields of wheat
- Generic "devotional blue" (the color every competitor uses)

## 4. The Six-Screenshot Sequence

### Format Notes
- Platform: iPhone 6.9" (iPhone 17 Pro) — 1320 × 2868px at 3x
- Orientation: Portrait only
- Frame style: Real device frame (Space Black or Natural Titanium — not mockup plastic)
- Headline overlay placement: Upper third of each frame, with the device screen filling the lower two-thirds
- Background of headline area: Near-black (`#0A0A0A`) with optional subtle grain texture

---

### Screenshot 1 — The Hook
**This is the one screenshot every person sees. It must earn the tap.**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~52pt):**
> The most personal
> Bible study in the world.

**Subhead (Inter Medium, 15pt, tracked):**
> A complete spiritual ecosystem for your walk with God.

**What to Show (Device Screen):**
The Today tab home screen. Key UI elements visible:
- The greeting at the top ("Good morning, [Name]")
- The current devotional series card showing a custom title (e.g., "Finding Rest in the Unfinished — Day 4 of 7") with the gold accent progress bar partially filled
- The midday check-in card below it with a message like "Quick pulse check. How's today landing?" and the five mood response options (sun, cloud, moon icons)
- The ember particle field visible in the background if the day's reading is completed (subtle warm gold particles rising)
- The custom tab bar at the bottom (Today, Bible, Ask, Journal) with frosted glass blur

**Design Direction:**
The headline is the star here. The UI underneath should be clearly readable but the headline does the work. The key visual idea to communicate: this app knows this specific person's name and this specific series title is not "30 Days of Hope" — it is something personal and specific that no one else has.

**Conversion Note:**
Do not use a placeholder series title like "My Devotional Series." Use a specific, evocative title that makes the viewer think "that sounds like it was written for someone real." Something like "When Plans Don't Go as Planned — Day 3 of 5" or "For the Grief You Don't Talk About — Day 6 of 9."

---

### Screenshot 2 — The Mechanism
**This is where you show the "how" to viewers who tapped through.**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~46pt):**
> Tell it your story.
> Get devotionals
> *written for you.*

**Subhead (Inter Medium, 14pt):**
> Not a template. Your story, your struggles, your series.

**What to Show (Device Screen):**
The onboarding or the "Your Story" generation moment. Two options — pick the stronger one visually:

Option A: An onboarding screen where the user is answering a prompt like "What's weighing on you most right now?" with a text response visible (partially written, feels real and specific — e.g., "My relationship with my dad has been strained since..."). The gold accent pulsing on the active text field.

Option B: The generation in-progress screen — the shimmer loading state while the first day is being written, with a status message like "Writing your first devotional..." overlaid on the near-black background. More dramatic, communicates the live AI generation.

**Design Direction:**
This is the moment that separates Unfold from every other app. The viewer is supposed to feel: "Wait — it actually uses what I type?" Make the personal text in Option A feel real and vulnerable. It should not say "I'm feeling stressed about work." It should say something that a real person might type at 6am when they are actually struggling.

**This is the conceptual screenshot. It requires original graphic design — not a raw UI screenshot.**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~44pt):**

### Screenshot 4 — The Feature Depth (Bible + Ask)
**This is for the viewer who wants to know "is there more than just a devotional?"**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~44pt):**
> Every question.
> Every chapter.
> Right here.

**Subhead (Inter Medium, 14pt):**
> Full Bible reader. AI companion. 32 study methods.

**What to Show (Device Screen — Split or Carousel):**
Two panels showing complementary features:

Panel A (left or top): The Ask tab — a chat conversation where the user has asked a theological question, e.g., "What does Paul actually mean by 'the flesh' in Romans 8?" and the companion has responded with a 3–4 line answer that includes a verse reference shown as an inline pill (e.g., `Romans 8:5-8`). The UI is clean chat bubbles — user message on the right (soft cream background), companion response on the left (no bubble, just text in the Instrument Serif voice).

Panel B (right or bottom): The Bible reader in the BSB translation, showing a passage mid-scroll with a highlighted verse glowing in the accent gold.

**Design Direction:**
This screenshot is about breadth. The goal is to communicate: you do not need YouVersion and you do not need ChatGPT and you do not need a separate journal app. This is the whole thing in one place.

### Screenshot 5 — The Daily Ritual (Journal + Check-In)
**This is for the viewer who wants to know what daily engagement looks like.**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~44pt):**
> Morning reading.
> Midday check-in.
> Evening reflection.

**Subhead (Inter Medium, 14pt):**
> A rhythm that fits real life.

**What to Show (Device Screen — Stacked trio of cards or a single journal view):**
Two options:

Option A: Three stacked "micro-screenshots" showing the three daily touch points — the devotional reading card (morning), the midday check-in card with mood selector visible, and the journal SOAP entry screen. Presented as overlapping cards in the frame, suggesting the three-moment daily rhythm.

Option B: The SOAP journal screen showing all four sections (Scripture / Observation / Application / Prayer) with real-looking text filled in at least one section. The "Go Deeper" AI button visible at the bottom with the gold accent.

**Design Direction:**
The key message here is routine, not overwhelm. The viewer should feel: "That is actually doable. That fits into my day." Avoid showing everything at once. Three simple things, presented calmly.

---

### Screenshot 6 — The Investment Closer
**This is the conversion screenshot for viewers who have made it to the end.**

**Headline (Instrument Serif, ~44pt):**
> Your complete spiritual
> ecosystem. $8.99/month.

**Subhead (Inter Medium, 14pt):**
> Personalized. Adaptive. Entirely yours.

**What to Show (Device Screen):**
The "You" profile or stats screen, showing:
- Current streak (a number like 14 or 21 — something that feels earned)
- The active devotional series with progress
- A "Past Devotionals" section showing 2–3 completed series titles (with the gold checkmark or completion indicator)
- Optionally: the PDF export preview thumbnail

Alternatively, use the streak + series completion celebration screen (the StreakCelebration or CompletionCelebration animation — frozen at its most visually satisfying frame).

**Design Direction:**
The emotional beat here is investment and accumulation. The viewer sees evidence that people build something real with this app over time. The specific number chosen for the streak matters — 14 days is believable. 3 days looks like churn. 200 days looks like an outlier. 14–28 is the sweet spot.

The price in the headline is intentional. Research consistently shows that showing a low price in the final screenshot removes a conversion barrier. Do not put the price in screenshot 1 — it anchors too early. Screenshot 6 is the right place.

## 5. The Rhythm Circle — Specification

This is an original graphic that needs to be designed. It appears in Screenshot 3 and can be repurposed for the App Store feature graphic, website, and any marketing materials.

### What It Communicates
The Unfold adaptive loop: the user's daily actions feed forward into tomorrow's devotional content. This is not a static product — it is a living system that remembers.

### The Four Nodes
The circle has four labeled nodes arranged at approximately 12 o'clock, 3 o'clock, 6 o'clock, and 9 o'clock positions:

1. **Daily Devotional** (top, 12 o'clock)
   - Label: "Daily Devotional"
   - Sub-label: "Generated for you, fresh each morning"
   - Icon suggestion: open book or sun

2. **Journal + Reflection** (right, 3 o'clock)
   - Label: "Journal & Reflection"
   - Sub-label: "SOAP method, free write, Go Deeper questions"
   - Icon suggestion: pencil or book with bookmark

3. **Midday & Evening Check-Ins** (bottom, 6 o'clock)
   - Label: "Check-Ins"
   - Sub-label: "How's your heart today? How did it land?"
   - Icon suggestion: sun/moon or pulse

4. **Ask the Companion** (left, 9 o'clock)
   - Label: "Ask"
   - Sub-label: "Biblical questions, deeper dives, any time"
   - Icon suggestion: chat bubble

### The Center
The center of the circle contains:
- The Unfold wordmark or a simple flame/ember icon in the signature gold
- Label text: "Shapes tomorrow's content"

### The Arrows
Curved directional arrows connecting each node to the next, flowing clockwise. The arrows should be subtle — thin stroked curves, not thick bold arrows. Arrowheads should be small and clean. The overall direction of travel is clockwise, suggesting an ongoing, self-reinforcing cycle.

### The "Tomorrow" Callout
Above or near the top node (Daily Devotional), add a subtle annotation that reads: "Tomorrow's devotional is being shaped right now." This is the emotional punch — the reader realizes the system is alive.

### Visual Style
- Background: `#0A0A0A` (matches app)
- Node circles: `rgba(245, 240, 235, 0.06)` fill, `rgba(245, 240, 235, 0.18)` stroke — the "glass" style from the app
- Node labels: Instrument Serif for main label, Inter for sub-label
- Arrows: `#C8A55C` (gold) at 60% opacity — they should glow subtly, not scream
- Center element: Full `#C8A55C` gold for the icon/wordmark
- Overall feel: The diagram in a beautifully designed product teardown piece — informational but warm

### What to Avoid
- Do not make this feel like a corporate slide deck circular diagram
- Do not use flat primary colors (blue/green/red for each node) — monochrome with the gold accent only
- Do not add drop shadows or 3D effects — keep it flat and precise
- The circle should feel like it was drawn by hand once and then refined — not generated by a template

## 6. Positioning Notes (Internal — Not for Screenshots)

These notes are for the designer to understand the emphasis. **Do not name or compare to any competitor in the screenshots.**

- Unfold's differentiation is specificity — show UI that is visibly specific to one person's story. Proper nouns in series titles matter. "Finding Rest in the Unfinished" beats "A Devotional Series."
- Unfold covers the full daily devotional ritual in one app: reading, journaling, reflection, AI-assisted questions, and Bible lookup. Screenshot 4 (the feature depth shot) must communicate this completeness.
- The rhythm circle in Screenshot 3 is the visual argument for the adaptive loop: your input changes the output. No other approach can make this claim.
- Unfold must communicate warmth and structure, not raw AI generation. The SOAP journal, the Bible reader, the streak — these are devotional-first product decisions. Screenshots should feel human and earned, not algorithmic.

## 7. Conversion Optimization Notes

### Screenshots 1–2 Are the Entire Funnel
App Store analytics consistently show that 60–80% of visitors never swipe past screenshot 2 before making their decision. Screenshots 3–6 convert the person who is already interested. Screenshots 1–2 must close everyone else.

**Screenshot 1 must answer: "Is this for me?"**
The series title shown in the UI must feel recognizably personal. If the viewer can imagine themselves or someone they know being that series — "When Plans Don't Go as Planned" — they tap. If it reads as generic Christian content, they scroll on.

**Screenshot 2 must answer: "How does it know what to write?"**
The onboarding moment or generation moment must make the mechanism legible in 2 seconds. Viewers should immediately understand: I type something, it writes something for me. Not: "this app has a lot of features."

### Screenshot Sequence Psychology
The sequence follows a classic sales structure:
1. Headline / Hook — make the claim
2. Mechanism — prove the claim
3. Differentiation — show why no one else can make the claim
4. Breadth — show there is more here than one trick
5. Routine — show how it fits daily life
6. Price close — remove the remaining objection

### Text Density
Limit each screenshot to one headline, one subhead, and one visible UI screen. No bullet lists. No feature checklist overlays. No "5 things you get" slides. Single idea per frame.

### The "Social Proof" Gap
These six screenshots do not include social proof. If a version 2 of this set is created after accumulating ratings, consider replacing Screenshot 6 with a social proof variant: a pull quote from a real review (with star rating), shown over a beautiful background with the series completion screen below. "I've tried every devotional app. This one stuck." — that kind of thing. Hold this for v2 when the rating count is high enough to be credible.

### App Preview Video
After completing the six static screenshots, the next highest-leverage asset is an App Preview video (15–30 seconds). The suggested storyboard: open with the onboarding prompt typing moment (5s), cut to the reading screen as text appears to generate (5s), cut to the check-in card midday notification (4s), cut to the journal SOAP screen (4s), close on the home screen with the series card and ember particles glowing (5s), fade to the wordmark on black with the tagline. No voiceover needed — music and motion carry it.
