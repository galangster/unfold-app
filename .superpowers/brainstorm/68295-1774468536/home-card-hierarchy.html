Zone 1 — Header
Greeting + Name + Streak + Avatar
Why it's here: Personal identity anchoring. Seeing your name + streak immediately reinforces "this is mine" and "I'm building something."

Why this size: Compact. The header should never compete with the hero card. Name is large (28px display), but everything else is muted.

Ambient detail: Subtle concentric circle SVGs (inspired by Endel's moving geometry). Nearly invisible — they create atmosphere, not distraction. In production these would slowly rotate via Reanimated.
Zone 2 — Daily Bridge
AI-generated transition from yesterday to today
Why it's here: Continuity. This is the feature that makes Unfold feel like a living relationship, not a content feed. It references yesterday's check-in + today's theme. Already in the codebase (bridge-service.ts).

Why this size: Small, inline bubble. It's a nudge, not a headline. Compact orb + speech bubble pattern — feels like a friend texting you.

Conditional: Only appears on Day 2+. Day 1 users don't see it. Disappears once they tap into reading.
Zone 3 — Hero Devotional Card ★
THE primary action — today's reading
Why it's here: This is what 90%+ of users open the app for. It MUST be the most prominent element, immediately tappable. Hero card = hero action.

Why this size: Largest card on screen (~45% of visible viewport). Rich content: title, scripture teaser, study method badge, progress bar, CTA button. Everything you need to decide "yes, I'm reading today" without scrolling.

Ambient detail: Concentric circle SVG art in the top-right corner. In production, these would slowly pulse/rotate via React Native Skia or Reanimated — creating that "living" Endel feel without being distracting.

Design principle: The gold accent border + subtle gradient background + shadow elevation all work together to make this feel elevated above everything else. It's the only card with an AccentGlow CTA button.
Zone 4 — Discovery Grid
Streak + Companion + Bible Reader — varied card sizes
Why it's here: Secondary actions. After the hero card, these are the next most common things people do: check their streak, ask the companion something, or read the Bible directly.

Card sizing logic (Endel-inspired):
• Streak (small square, left): Glanceable — you just need the number. No tap needed unless curious.
• Companion (small square, right): Entry point to Ask tab. Small because it's available as a full tab — this is a shortcut, not the destination.
• Bible Reader (full width): Wider because it carries context ("Continue in John 14"). The horizontal layout with chevron invites a tap.

Why varied sizes: Visual rhythm. Same-sized cards create monotony (an AI slop tell). Different sizes create hierarchy within the grid — your eye knows what to scan first.
Zone 5 — Remember This
Random highlight from past devotionals
Why it's here: Delight + reinforcement. Surfacing a past highlight makes the user feel "my history matters here." It's the same mechanic as Kindle's daily highlight email — low effort, high emotional payoff.

Why this size: Medium — full width but compact height. The colored left border draws the eye without competing with the hero card. Only appears if user has highlights (progressive disclosure).

Already built: RememberThisCard.tsx exists. Just needs repositioning from current layout.
Zone 6 — Your Series
Library of past & in-progress series
Why it's here: Lowest priority on home — users access their library infrequently (1-2x/week). But it's important for the feeling of "I've built something here" and for switching between series.

Why this size: Compact horizontal scroll cards. Takes minimal vertical space but shows breadth. "See All" links to the full library in the You tab.

Already built: YourSeriesSection.tsx exists as a vertical list — would change to horizontal scroll for space efficiency.
On the Endel-Inspired Ambient Art
The concentric circles and line art throughout are static in this mockup. In the real app, these would be:

• React Native Skia for GPU-rendered geometry
• Slow rotation (0.5-2rpm) with Reanimated withRepeat
• Very low opacity (3-5%) — felt, not seen
• Theme-reactive — accent-colored, so they shift with the user's chosen theme

The goal is the same as Endel: the screen feels alive without anything specific demanding attention. Beauty through subtlety.
