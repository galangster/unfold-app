Audio Player: What should "dismiss" mean?

Currently, tapping the X button destroys the audio — no way to bring it back without re-generating via TTS. Let's define the right behavior.

Current Problems

  • Closing the player destroys the audio URI (can't restore)
  • No swipe gesture — only the small X button
  • Opening a study method kills the audio too
  • No way to minimize/hide without stopping

What should closing the player do?

Pick the model that feels right for a devotional listening experience

A

Spotify Model — Hide, keep playing

Swipe down or tap X to hide the bar. Audio keeps playing in the background. A small "headphones" icon in the header lets you bring it back. Explicit "stop" requires long-press or a second action.

Pros

  • Audio never accidentally lost
  • Can read while listening
  • Familiar pattern

Cons

  • More complex state management
  • Devotionals are short (~2 min) — may be overkill
  • Background audio needs careful cleanup
B

Podcast Model — Pause + minimize, restore from header

Swipe down to minimize (pauses audio, hides bar). A subtle indicator in the reading header shows paused audio is available. Tap to restore and resume. X button fully stops and clears.

Pros

  • Two tiers: minimize (recoverable) vs close (final)
  • Clear user intent — swipe = temporary, X = done
  • No background audio complexity

Cons

  • Two different dismiss actions might confuse
  • Need a restore indicator somewhere
C

Simple Model — Close means close, but cache the position

X button and swipe both close and pause. But the playback position is cached. Next time user taps "Listen", it resumes from where they left off instead of restarting. No minimize/restore — just smart resume.

Pros

  • Simplest to implement and understand
  • No hidden state or restore UX
  • Smart resume feels polished

Cons

  • Can't listen while scrolling through text
  • No background playback
